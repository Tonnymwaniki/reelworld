What Reel does

Reel is an archival assistant designed to connect conversational human memories with an open photographic cadastre of Kenya. Instead of matching vague keywords or guessing, Reel analyzes physical facades, verified landmarks, and legible street inscriptions to retrieve real photographs that substantiate what you describe.

Deterministic Facade Indexing

Reel also bridges your own personal photographs into the public collection—revealing sightlines, complementary vantage points, and shared architectural volumes without indexing personal identities or compromising privacy.

Where Reel can be wrong

Reel reports uncertainty openly. Here are the boundaries where automated visual recovery can falter:

Boundary 01

Signs read from far away

Extreme camera perspective angles, heavy atmospheric dust, or distant signage characters can result in partial or mistaken OCR transcriptions. Reel marks these as 'Partial' candidate readings.

info Tagged as Partial Candidate
Boundary 02

Places named from small clues

Isolated architectural motifs (such as a generic concrete lintel or standard kerbstone) without corroborating surrounding context may match multiple candidate sites across the city.

help Requires Multi-Plate Corroboration
Boundary 03

People counts in crowds

Dense pedestrian thoroughfares and overlapping street silhouettes prevent precise spatial separation; Reel explicitly avoids speculative enumeration in crowded market vistas.

block Speculative Count Suppressed
